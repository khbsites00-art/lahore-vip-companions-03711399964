The Setting

What Makes a Good Outcall Setting

Clients tell us the same handful of things made their visit comfortable. None of them cost money.

Privacy first

A quiet room beats a large one. Close the corridor light, turn the television down and agree on the situation with anyone you live with. Guests who sort this out before dispatch enjoy the evening. Guests who sort it out at the door do not.

Light that flatters

Bright ceiling light makes everyone tense. A lamp in the corner does more for the mood than any decoration. Clear a path from the door to a seat, and leave the room lit well enough to see a face clearly.

Low noise

Barking dogs, loud music and a shouting neighbour break the rhythm fast. Choose the quietest hour your building allows. Ground floor flats near a main road need the windows shut. Late slots after midnight are naturally calmer across Lahore.

Easy access

Tell us about tight lanes, broken lifts, parked cars blocking a gate and which floor you sit on. A fifth floor walk-up in Iqbal Town adds five minutes to the window. We would rather know every detail than guess at a dark gate.

Something to offer

Water poured and waiting is the standard courtesy. Tea or a soft drink is better. A clean bathroom with fresh towels matters more than people expect, and a made bed says everything about how seriously you take the visit.

Phones on silent

Nothing ends an evening faster than a phone buzzing every four minutes. Put both phones face down, pause notifications and close the group chat. Two hours of undivided attention is what you paid for, so take it.

The setting is half the experience. The other half is the person you choose, so spend a minute on the profile before you spend two hours in the room.

Host Etiquette

The Host's Checklist Before She Arrives

A companion crosses half of Lahore to reach your door. Five minutes of preparation keeps the visit smooth for both of you.

Run through this list before you call, not after she has left. It is short, it costs almost nothing, and it is the difference between a good evening and an awkward one.

  • Cash counted once, in an envelope. We take cash on arrival only, so have the agreed figure ready before she knocks.
  • Directions that a driver can follow. House number, street, landmark and any guard code, all in one message.
  • A clean bathroom and fresh towels. It takes four minutes and it sets the tone for everything after it.
  • Water poured, room tidied, bed made. Small signs of respect are read instantly by a professional.
  • Phones silenced and valuables put away. Trust is built slowly, and a tidy room removes doubt in seconds.
  • Your schedule cleared for the full block. Add ten minutes either side so a delayed lift never eats into your time.
  • Light perfume, used lightly. Strong scent fills a small room quickly and clings to clothing afterwards.

Etiquette runs both ways. She will arrive dressed for a hotel corridor rather than a bedroom, and she will leave when the block ends. Greet her at the door, confirm the length of the booking and settle the payment before you sit down. Offer water first. Ask before taking any photograph, and accept the answer you get. Our rates page lists every fee so there is no negotiation at your door.

Guests who treat the visit like a professional appointment get the best evenings. Guests who treat it like a transaction get a shorter one, and they are rarely invited back.

Honest Advice

Mistakes Guests Make With Outcall

We have taken bookings since 2019. These seven errors account for most of the evenings that go wrong.

Calling when she should already be there. Matching takes a few minutes and travel takes twenty more. Guests who call at the exact moment they want company lose the first half hour of their own block. Ring before you need her.

Skipping the address details. A house number without a street, a hotel name without a room, a flat without a floor. Vague addresses cost ten minutes at every gate in Lahore. Send the whole string in one message, then a pin on WhatsApp.

Sending money in advance. This is the most expensive mistake in the market, and it does not only happen to careless people. We never request a transfer. Cash at the door, every time. Anyone pressing you for payment before travel is not from this page.

Under-booking the block. Two hours looks generous on paper and feels short in the room, especially when dinner runs long. Book three hours if the evening has a second half. Agreed extensions cost less than a rushed one at the end.

Ignoring hotel policy. Some properties charge for a visitor or ask guests to sign in. Learn the rule before you book, not at the front desk at midnight. Tell us the hotel and we will say honestly how easy it is.

Ringing every ten minutes. She cannot answer while the car is moving through traffic. One call when she is close tells you everything you need. Repeated calls pull her attention off the road and start the evening badly.

Treating the companion like a purchase. Rudeness ends a booking early, and we will not send anyone back to that address. Respect, clear boundaries and a clean space are the entire etiquette. They cost nothing at all.

When outcall is not the right choice

Outcall is not for every night. If you share a flat with relatives who notice guests, if your building forbids visitors, or if you want complete anonymity at the door, an incall suits you better than a companion arriving at your address. The same applies if your only time slot is thirty minutes after a long flight, because travel and matching will not fit. An incall booking removes the travel fee and the gate conversation entirely. Tell us the constraint on the call and we will point you to the format that fits, even when the answer is not outcall.
