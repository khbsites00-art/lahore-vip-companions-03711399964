Model Town as a Neighbourhood: Blocks, Boulevards and Township Character

Model Town was drawn up in 1921 as a cooperative garden suburb. The plan followed the garden-city idea, and the founding meeting was held in Lahore's Town Hall. A century later you can still read the plan from the driver's seat. Wide roads cut the society into a clean square grid. The core blocks run A through H, then J and K. There is no I block, and residents will tell you so before you ask.

Beyond that ring sit the extension blocks L, M, N, P, Q, R and S. The Lahore Development Authority administers them. They are newer, the plots are larger in places, and the streets are a little less polished. Knowing which block you are heading to matters. It changes your arrival time, your parking and how quietly you can pull up.

A few landmarks do most of the work. Club Chowk in F Block holds the society office. Bank Square Market in C Block is the busiest commercial pocket, with banks, pharmacies and cafes side by side. The circular Model Town Park sits near the middle, and the library is a short walk from it. Model Town Club runs along Circular Road. Give a companion "Bank Square, C Block" and she will find you without a second call.

The character is old money and mature trees. Bungalows behind low walls. Footpaths that people actually use. Guards sitting at block gates. Families out walking after sunset. It is a family society first, and everything about an evening here follows from that. Quiet lanes keep conversation private. They also mean a new face gets noticed within minutes. Both facts work in your favour if you behave like you belong.

One confusion worth clearing up. Township is a separate neighbourhood just beyond Model Town's edge, not a part of it. Drivers mix the two up regularly. If your address says Township, you are a few minutes further out, and the travel estimate changes.
