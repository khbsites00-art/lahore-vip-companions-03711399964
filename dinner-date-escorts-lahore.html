Etiquette Tips for Dinner Dates

Etiquette is not about impressing anyone. It is about removing friction so the evening can be enjoyable. These points come straight from feedback clients and companions have given us since 2019.

Respect and Courtesy

Your companion is a guest at your table, not a prop for it. Ask her opinion, listen to the answer, and let silences sit when they arrive naturally. Companions give better company to clients who treat them like people. That is not sentiment, it is how the evening improves.

Communication Beforehand

Say what you want from the night. Quiet and slow, or lively with a group. Any topic you would rather not touch. Whether you plan to stay for one course or four. Five minutes of clarity on WhatsApp saves an awkward correction at the table.

Punctuality

Tables get released after fifteen minutes in most busy rooms. Arrive on time or call ahead. If you are running late, a quick message lets your companion wait somewhere sensible instead of standing at an entrance. Companions extend the same courtesy to you.

Your Own Appearance

She will have made an effort. You should match it. A clean shirt, shoes that are not worn out, and a shave cover almost everything. The pair gets judged together by every waiter who walks past, and you feel better when the judgment lands in your favour.

Conversation and Table Talk

Good conversation is the actual product of a dinner date. It is also the part clients worry about most, which is strange, because it is the easiest part to fix.

Openers That Work

Ask what she has been reading, watching or cooking. Ask about the last trip she took. Ask what she thinks of the restaurant, the neighbourhood, or the dish you just ordered. Questions with a real answer beat questions that close with a yes.

Share something first. A short story about your week, a piece of work you are proud of, a place you keep meaning to revisit. People open up when someone else goes first.

Topics to Park

Skip your last relationship. Skip detailed complaints about your ex or your staff. Skip anything that asks her to act as a therapist. Politics and religion are fine with people who enjoy them, but they sour a table fast when either side feels cornered.

Never ask for names, numbers or addresses of other clients. Never ask a companion to confirm anything about another customer. It is a firm rule with us, and clients who push on it get one polite warning.

Reading the Room

If she gives a one-line answer twice in a row, the topic is dead. Change it rather than pushing. If she leans in and asks you a follow-up, you have found the thread. Follow it and let the meal run long.

Put the phone face down. Checking it every four minutes tells the table you would rather be elsewhere, and everyone present can feel it.

When a Dinner Date Isn't the Right Format

We turn people away from this format regularly. Better to say it now than after money has changed hands.

You Want Something Instant

A dinner date needs a venue, a reservation and two to three hours. If you want company in the next twenty minutes, this is the wrong product and the wrong expectation. Somebody promising otherwise is either new or dishonest.

You Are Looking for a Public Debut

Bringing a companion to a family dinner, a wedding where your relatives will ask questions, or an event with your staff is a decision with consequences. We will do it if you insist and if the room can handle it. We will also tell you honestly when we think it will not end well.

Your Budget Is Below the Band

Dinner companionship starts at PKR 25,000 for a reason. If that is above what you planned to spend, book a shorter daytime meeting instead, or wait until the budget is there. Discounted offers from strangers online are almost always a trap.

You Expect Guaranteed Chemistry

Nobody can promise that two people will click. We match on interests, tone and occasion, and most evenings go well. Some do not, and both people are polite about it through dessert. That is the honest risk in any social booking, and anyone promising certainty is selling something else.

You Are Not Comfortable in Public

If the idea of being seen at a table with someone you hired makes you tense, the tension will show. Choose a quiet room with a private booth, or choose a different service. Nervous clients have a worse time than they need to.
