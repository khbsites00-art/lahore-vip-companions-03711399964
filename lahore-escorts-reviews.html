Our Method

How We Collect Client Feedback

Every quote on this page started as a private message to our concierge. We have asked for feedback after bookings since 2019. The request usually goes out on WhatsApp the morning after an encounter. A client can ignore it, and many do. Those who reply shape this page.

We never pay for a review. No discount, no free hour, no upgrade in exchange for a flattering sentence. A client who writes to us pays the same rate as everybody else. That rule has not moved in seven years of trading in Lahore.

Names are shortened or dropped before anything is published. Areas stay only when the client agreed to them. We never attach photographs, phone numbers, hotel room numbers, or workplace hints to a quote. What remains is a message, not a dossier.

What we edit, and what we do not

Long messages sometimes get trimmed for length. The opinion behind the words never changes. If a companion arrived late, that line stays. If a client found the first half hour stiff, that line stays too. Readers can tell when praise has been scrubbed clean, and they stop trusting it.

Some feedback arrives unprompted. Clients write to us when something breaks, and when a companion exceeds expectations. Those messages enter the same pool as the ones we ask for. Nothing on this page is drafted by a marketing team, then signed off by a fictional customer.

How themes are counted

Messages are grouped into themes every quarter. Praise, complaints, and suggestions are counted separately. The themes below come from that tally, not from a hand-picked set of flattering quotes. Volume matters less than honesty. Forty blunt messages teach us more than four hundred hollow ones.

Every message is matched against our booking log before it is used. That check confirms a booking happened on the stated date. It is an internal check, and we describe it that way. We are not an independent auditor, and no outside rating platform stands behind the figures on this page.

Fixed Over Time

Recurring Complaints and How We Fixed Them

Some complaints repeat. When a message keeps coming back, the fix has to sit in the process, not in an apology. These four shaped how the agency runs today.

Complaint one: profile photos went stale

Clients reported that a gallery image was older than the companion's current look. We moved to a monthly photo sweep and pulled images that could not be reconfirmed. The screening check now includes a dated capture taken in person. Photo accuracy has been the most praised point in feedback ever since.

Complaint two: pricing changed mid-booking

Two years ago a companion would quote a travel add-on at the door. Clients hated it, and they were right. We now confirm every component in writing before anyone travels: base time, travel band, hotel arrangement and any late-hour adjustment. What you read in the message is what you pay in cash.

Complaint three: nobody answered after midnight

Late bookings used to sit unanswered until morning. The concierge desk now runs overnight coverage, and the phone line stays staffed. If a message goes quiet for more than fifteen minutes, call instead. A voice answer beats a waiting screen.

Complaint four: repeat clients were matched from scratch

Regulars grew tired of explaining the same preferences every time. We added a preference log keyed to the booking number, covering timing, conversation style, hotel habits and boundaries. On a repeat booking the concierge reads it back to you before dispatching anyone.

None of these changes came from a review site. They came from our own inbox, collected since 2019, and from clients who bothered to complain instead of simply leaving. Complaints are the cheapest research we ever get.

How the satisfaction figure works

After a booking, the concierge asks three plain questions: did the companion arrive as arranged, did she match the profile, and would you book again. Clients who answer the survey can leave a score out of five. We average those self-reported scores and round to one decimal. A small sample in a quiet month can move it by a tenth. Treat the number as a direction, not a precision instrument.

Repeat means a second paid booking within ninety days of the first, matched on the booking number. It excludes our own follow-up messages and it excludes reschedules made by the concierge. Clients who move to WhatsApp-only contact after the first booking still count when they return.

How to Spot Fake Review Patterns Anywhere Else

This section has nothing to do with us and everything to do with you. Fake reviews are common across Lahore's adult services market, and the same tells appear almost everywhere once you know them. Use the checklist on any site you visit, including this one.

The burst pattern

Twenty glowing reviews posted across two days, then silence for six months, is a campaign rather than a customer base. Real feedback arrives unevenly, because real bookings do. Check dates before you check stars.

Identical phrasing

Reviews that use the same adjectives in the same order, or repeat the agency name three times in one paragraph, were written by one person. Different clients notice different things. Uniform praise is a warning sign.

No local detail

Genuine Lahore clients mention the neighbourhood, the hotel lobby, the traffic, the hour. Reviews that could have been written for any city in the world carry no information. Specificity is expensive to fake.

Perfect scores with no texture

Every service has bad nights. A page with zero criticism is either very young or heavily filtered. Look for the reviews that complain, then read how the agency answers them. The reply tells you more than the rating does.

Pressure to pay first

The most expensive review is the one that convinces you to send an advance. No amount of online praise replaces a cash-on-arrival arrangement, where you verify the person before money changes hands. Read our safety notes before you commit anywhere.

Apply these tests to every directory, gallery and agency page you open. A five-minute check saves a bad booking and a worse story.

Verification

Verify the Companion Before You Judge Any Review

Reviews describe somebody else's evening. Verification protects yours. Work through these steps first, then let the feedback inform the choice rather than drive it.

Ask for a fresh photo

Before you agree anything, request a photo taken that day with a hand gesture you name. A current image arrives within minutes. A stock image does not. This single question removes most of the risk in the market.

Confirm age and consent in writing

Every companion we introduce is eighteen or over, and identification is checked at screening. Ask the concierge to confirm it for your booking and keep the message. Any hesitation on that question is your answer to walk away.

Set the terms before anyone travels

Agree the arrival window, the exact figure including travel, and the fact that payment happens on arrival. Keep all three in one thread. If terms shift at the door, the booking ends there.

Meet on ground you control

For a first booking, an outcall to a hotel you booked yourself keeps the setting in your hands. Our booking guide walks through the sequence, and our safety page covers the risks without softening them.

Then read the reviews for patterns

Once the practical checks pass, feedback becomes useful. Read it for repeated themes rather than single verdicts. Three mentions of punctuality tell you more than one paragraph of adjectives, because a pattern survived several different evenings.

Verification takes about ten minutes. It is the ten minutes that matter, and no star rating can do it for you.

By Booking Type

Client Feedback by Booking Type

The same agency gets judged differently depending on what was booked. We tag every message with the booking type, and the themes split cleanly. Here is what clients say about each of the three formats we handle most.

Dinner dates

Dinner bookings draw the longest messages. Clients judge conversation, dress, and how naturally a companion handled a public room. The compliments centre on company rather than logistics, because a restaurant puts two people at a table for two hours. Criticism, when it appears, is about conversation running thin or an arrival cut close to the reservation time. Book the dinner companion twenty minutes earlier than you think you need, and say whether the evening is formal or relaxed when you call.

Hotel and outcall bookings

Here the writing turns practical. Clients mention check-in ease, how discreet the arrival looked, and whether the companion moved through a lobby without drawing attention. Read the hotel booking notes before you reserve. Clients who arrange room access first, then book, report much smoother evenings than those who improvise at the front desk. Security desks in central Lahore ask questions, and an unprepared arrival costs you the first twenty minutes.

Full-night bookings

A full night tests temperament more than anything else. Clients write about whether the companion stayed engaging past midnight, respected agreed boundaries, and kept the room relaxed. Complaints in this format are usually about tiredness on either side rather than conduct. The full-night arrangement suits clients who value steady company over a fixed script. Say your sleep habits upfront if you want the evening shaped around them.

Short daytime bookings

Two-hour daytime requests draw comments about punctuality above all else. Clients have a train, a meeting or a flight afterwards, so a late start ruins the format. We keep a traffic buffer on daytime windows for that reason, and clients notice the difference.

Whatever the format, three standards stay constant: a verified profile, clear terms before anyone travels, and cash on arrival. Pick the format that fits the evening and let the concierge handle the rest.
