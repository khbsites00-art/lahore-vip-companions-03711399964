Scope of Service

What a Business Companion Does, and What She Does Not

A corporate companion is a professional guest, not a member of your staff. She is hired for her presence, her manners and her conversation. Clients tell us the smoothest evenings are the ones where expectations were settled before anyone arrived. Ours are settled right here.

Business owners in Gulberg and DHA often assume a companion needs no direction. That is rarely true. Two minutes of instruction on arrival changes the whole table. Her job is to follow your lead, not to invent one.

What your companion does

  • She arrives 10 to 15 minutes early and waits at the meeting point you chose.
  • She dresses to the code you confirmed, and checks nothing with you in public.
  • She introduces herself by first name and keeps the introduction short.
  • She talks with your clients, hosts and colleagues, then reads the room and steps back.
  • She supports your networking by opening conversations and giving you a graceful exit.
  • She remembers the names and details you gave her in the brief.
  • She stays for the hours you booked, then leaves on time without fuss.

That covers a dinner for four, a hall full of delegates, or a three-day conference schedule. If the evening runs long, she can usually extend. We confirm extensions by message, so nobody is guessing about hours or money.

What she does not do

  • She will not speak for your company, negotiate a deal or sign anything for you.
  • She will not argue with staff, guests or drivers, even if the evening turns awkward.
  • She will not post photos, tag you or mention the booking to anyone afterwards.
  • She will not accept gifts, room upgrades or cash from your guests.
  • She will not stay past the booked hours without a fresh agreement.
  • She will not join a private after-party that was never in the brief.
  • She will not discuss your competitors, your marriage or your finances with anyone.

Boundaries keep an evening pleasant for both sides. When nobody has to guess, the conversation relaxes and your guests notice only that the table feels easy.

Conferences, Dinners, Galas and Launches in Detail

Corporate events in Lahore fall into four busy formats. Each one asks something different of your companion. Knowing the format helps us match the right person and the right preparation to your night. Tell us which one you face and we will start there.

Conferences and summits

Trade fairs and industry summits run at the Expo Centre in Johar Town, with smaller seminars in Gulberg and along Mall Road. Multi-day events start early and finish at a delegate dinner. Your companion attends the social programme: the welcome reception, chosen coffee breaks, and the closing dinner. She remembers the names you mention, so you are not repeating your introduction at hour six.

Long badge-wearing days reward a calm, well-rested presence more than a loud one. Breaks between sessions are where introductions happen, and where most delegates decide who is worth a second meeting. Book the three-day conference package if your schedule runs past two nights. Companions working that side of the city know Johar Town and its parking rules well.

Business dinners and client entertainment

Dinner is where much of the business in this city actually happens. Seating matters, and so does timing. Your companion sits where the host places her, keeps her phone silent, and never interrupts a negotiation. She understands local dining manners, from who orders first to when the guest of honour should be served.

If the table orders drinks, she keeps hers light and stays sharp. She also knows when a conversation needs air rather than another question. Guests remember whether the evening felt relaxed. They rarely remember the menu. That distinction is the whole point of booking well.

Award nights and charity galas

Black-tie events run on protocol. There is a receiving line, a seated dinner, speeches, and often a fundraising moment. Your companion walks in with you, handles the photograph line, and knows the difference between clapping at the right moment and drawing attention to herself. Pledges and auction paddles are your decision alone. She will never signal a number or bid on your behalf.

These evenings run past midnight more often than not. The eight-hour VIP Corporate tier suits them, because a tired companion at hour five is noticeable. Formal national dress sometimes replaces Western black tie at certain institutions, and we confirm that with you in advance.

Product launches and brand events

Launch nights are loud, scheduled to the minute, and usually covered by press. Your companion arrives briefed on the brand, any embargo rules, and the colour scheme she should respect. When cameras are near, she keeps the conversation on the product rather than on you. Organisers often ask every guest to sign a confidentiality clause, and companions sign without exception.

Queues at a photo wall move slowly. Staff are strict about timings and even stricter about guest lists. A companion who has worked launches before knows to eat before arrival, to keep a clutch small, and to stay off her phone while a presenter is speaking.

Trade shows and exhibitions

Exhibition days are repetitive. The same pitch is delivered forty times to people walking past your stand. A companion keeps energy consistent from the morning crowd to the afternoon lull, and she remembers faces you met hours earlier. She will never hand out your cards without asking, and she will never promise a follow-up you have not agreed. At closing time, most clients are surprised how much lighter the day felt.

Briefing Etiquette: Ten Minutes That Save the Evening

A good brief takes ten minutes. It is a short conversation, not a form. We keep a note for the booking and delete it after the event. Clients who skip this step usually call us twice on the night itself.

What to include

Give the event name, venue, start time and expected finish. Say what you need from her: conversation with clients, help while you make the rounds, or simply company at a table. Add three names she should recognise, with one line on what each person cares about. Flag topics to avoid, such as an internal dispute or a rival firm.

Note the dress code, any brand colours, and how you would like to be introduced. If a photographer will be present, say so. If the dinner includes a speech from you, tell her the timing so she is not mid-conversation when it starts. Small details decide how polished the evening looks from the outside.

What to leave out

Do not share passwords, bank details or your home address. She does not need your staff's private numbers either. Personal disputes stay outside the brief. If news is under embargo, share the fact of the embargo and the date, nothing more. Her non-disclosure agreement covers everything you do tell her, but less data means less risk for both sides.

The ten-minute call

A short call the day before beats a long message thread. Walk through the running order, the seating plan if you have one, and your exit time. Agree one signal for when you want rescuing from a conversation. On the night, a single message when you arrive is enough. Two minutes of feedback afterwards helps us do better next time.

Timing matters in this city. Call before 9pm so she can prepare, and avoid briefing an hour before doors open. Rushed briefings produce polite but shallow conversation, which is exactly what you are paying to avoid.
